&#x20;    INTRO

══════════════════════════════════════ -->

<p class="about-intro">

I am an environmental data scientist and ecosystem modeler with a Ph.D. in Environmental Dynamics (University of Arkansas, 2026). My work spans the full pipeline from field measurement and satellite remote sensing through machine learning, process-based modeling, and spatial analysis — applied to questions of carbon cycling, agricultural greenhouse gas emissions, land use change, and institutional decarbonization.

</p>



<p class="about-intro">

I thrive in interdisciplinary settings where quantitative models connect across sectors — coupling land, agriculture, climate, and human systems to support decision-making under uncertainty. I am drawn to roles where rigorous computational methods, open science practices, and collaborative research can inform decarbonization pathways, sustainability strategy, and environmental policy at multiple scales.

</p>



<div class="about-callout">

Recent work in <em>Nature</em> and <em>Nature Sustainability</em> highlights growing demand for environmental scientists who bridge scientific depth with applied quantitative methods in corporate, policy, and research settings —

(<a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s43017-024-00526-0" target="\_blank">Pavlov et al. 2024</a> · <a href="https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-04104-2" target="\_blank">Lee 2026</a>)

</div>
